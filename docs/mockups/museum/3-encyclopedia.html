    Nintendo, 4th Generation, home console

    Super Nintendo Entertainment System

    Nintendo’s 16-bit answer to the Mega Drive arrived late, then won the decade with its colour, sound and a library of classics.

    Written by AI on 2026-09-14. Rewrite this entry

    By 1990 Sega’s Mega Drive had a two-year head start. Nintendo answered with the Super Famicom in Japan that November, and the Super NES reached North America in August 1991 with Super Mario World in the box.

    Its graphics chip could scale and rotate a background layer, called Mode 7, which gave racing and flying games a sense of depth no other home console could match. Its Sony-designed sound chip made it the music machine of the era.

    Two years behind at launch, it still outsold its rival worldwide.

    One console, two looks New

    Japan and Europe got a rounded grey console with colourful buttons. North America got a boxier design with purple buttons. The games are the same, but cartridges are shaped differently, and European games often run slower because of the region’s TV standard.

    Legacy

    Nintendo kept making the console in Japan until 2003. Games such as A Link to the Past, Super Metroid and Chrono Trigger still top lists of the best games ever made.
